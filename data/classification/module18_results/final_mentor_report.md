# Document-Disjoint Multi-Label Classification of NDA Clauses Using Legal Language Models and Class-Imbalance-Aware Learning

**Comprehensive Technical Research & Evaluation Report**

---

## 1. Abstract

Automated clause classification in Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) poses severe technical challenges due to legal language heterogeneity, extreme multi-label category imbalance, and pseudo-label noise when relying on distant supervision from Large Language Models (LLMs). This study presents a rigorous empirical investigation of 14-category multi-label NDA clause classification across three transformer architectures (`Legal-RoBERTa`, `Legal-BERT`, `DeBERTa-v3`) and three loss functions (`BCE`, `Multi-Label Focal Loss`, `Class-Weighted BCE`) evaluated under strict document-disjoint split conditions (20 total contracts, 717 clauses; 12 train, 4 validation, 4 test; 0 document overlap). 

Ground-truth evaluation labels consist of distant-supervision pseudo-labels generated by a 3-model Gemini LLM ensemble (`gemini-1.5-pro`, `gemini-1.5-flash`, `gemini-1.0-pro`), rather than human-verified annotations. Among the initial 9-experiment matrix, **Legal-RoBERTa paired with Class-Weighted BCE (`EXP-03`)** emerged as the top-performing configuration, achieving a Test Macro F1 of **0.3675** at the default decision threshold of 0.50. A subsequent validation-only decision-threshold sweep identified **0.60** as optimal, which generalized to the frozen test split with a Test Macro F1 of **0.4295** (+0.0620 gain), Minority Macro F1 of **0.3853** (+0.1047 gain), and a 46.94% reduction in false positives (lowering Hamming Loss from 0.1967 to 0.1283). Pseudo-label confidence ablation revealed that training on all valid pseudo-labels (`ALL-VALID`, 344 clauses) outperformed confidence-filtered subsets (`HIGH+MEDIUM`, 201 clauses, Test Macro F1 = 0.2748), proving that retaining training volume and minority representation outweighs noise filtering under severe class imbalance.

## 2. Introduction

Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs) are among the most pervasive legal instruments in commercial transactions. Automated clause classification plays a crucial role in legal contract review, compliance auditing, and legal risk assessment. However, applying Natural Language Processing (NLP) to NDA clause text entails three distinct computational obstacles:

1. **Multi-Label Heterogeneity**: Single NDA clauses frequently convey concurrent legal functions (e.g. specifying party definitions alongside confidentiality scope). Multi-label classification is therefore strictly required.
2. **Extreme Class Imbalance**: High-frequency boilerplate categories (such as *Additional Information* or *Party Identification*) heavily outnumber rare specialized covenants (such as *Employees*, *NDA Type*, or *Governing Law*). Standard training causes models to collapse toward majority predictions.
3. **Template Overlap & Data Leakage**: Standard random clause splitting causes clauses from the same contract template to appear in both training and test sets, artificially inflating evaluation metrics.

To address these challenges, this project investigates domain-specific pretrained legal transformers (`saibo/legal-roberta-base`, `nlpaueb/legal-bert-base-uncased`) versus modern general-domain disentangled attention models (`microsoft/deberta-v3-base`) across imbalance-aware loss formulations under a strict document-disjoint splitting protocol.

## 3. Research Problem and Objectives

The central research problem investigated by this study is:

> *How do transformer model architectures, class-imbalance loss functions, pseudo-label confidence filtering, and decision threshold selection interact to dictate multi-label clause classification performance across frequent and minority NDA legal categories?*

The core objectives are:
1. **Architecture Benchmark (RQ1)**: Measure the relative performance of legal-domain vs general-domain transformer encoders for NDA clause classification.
2. **Imbalance-Aware Loss Evaluation (RQ2)**: Quantify the effect of Class-Weighted BCE and Multi-Label Focal Loss on minority class recall and overall Macro F1.
3. **Pseudo-Label Confidence Trade-off (RQ3)**: Evaluate whether filtering pseudo-labels by LLM ensemble agreement improves downstream classifier performance.
4. **Threshold Optimization & Minority Performance (RQ4)**: Investigate decision boundary optimization and analyze the statistical relationship between category support and F1 performance.

---

## 4. Research Questions and Hypotheses

This project evaluates four pre-registered research questions and hypotheses:

- **RQ1**: How do different transformer architectures perform for multi-label NDA clause classification under identical training conditions?
  - **H1**: Transformer model architecture will be associated with differences in multi-label NDA clause classification performance as measured by Macro F1.
- **RQ2**: How do different loss functions affect multi-label NDA clause classification performance, particularly for minority categories, under class imbalance?
  - **H2**: Loss functions designed to address class imbalance (Focal Loss, Class-Weighted BCE) will produce different per-category performance for minority categories compared with standard BCE loss.
- **RQ3**: How does pseudo-label confidence level affect downstream multi-label classification performance?
  - **H3**: Training using only high-confidence pseudo-labels will produce different downstream classification behavior compared with training using all valid pseudo-labels.
- **RQ4**: How consistently can the models classify minority NDA clause categories, and how does category support correlate with F1 performance?
  - **H4**: Classification performance will vary across the 14 NDA categories, with categories having lower positive support exhibiting lower F1 scores.

## 6. LLM-Based Pseudo-Labeling

Classification ground-truth labels consist of distant supervision **LLM pseudo-labels** generated by a 3-model Gemini ensemble (`gemini-1.5-pro`, `gemini-1.5-flash`, `gemini-1.0-pro`).

- **Pseudo-label Ground Truth**: These labels do not represent human-annotated legal ground truth. All evaluation metrics measure model alignment against the distant supervision LLM pseudo-labeling scheme.
- **Confidence Tiers**:
  - `HIGH_CONFIDENCE`: Agreement score = 1.0, confidence >= 0.8 (161 training clauses, 46.80%)
  - `MEDIUM_CONFIDENCE`: Agreement score >= 0.5, confidence >= 0.6 (40 training clauses, 11.63%)
  - `DISAGREEMENT`: Ensemble model conflict (111 training clauses, 32.27%)
  - `LOW_CONFIDENCE`: Ensemble confidence < 0.6 (32 training clauses, 9.30%)

## 7. Experimental Methodology

The study evaluates a controlled 3 x 3 experiment matrix (9 experiments):

- **Model Architectures**: `Legal-RoBERTa`, `Legal-BERT`, `DeBERTa-v3`
- **Loss Functions**: `BCEWithLogitsLoss`, `Multi-Label Focal Loss`, `Class-Weighted BCE`
- **Training Hyperparameters**:
  - Epochs: 5 (Early stopping patience = 3 on Validation Macro F1)
  - Batch Size: 8, Learning Rate: `2e-5`, Warmup: 10%, Weight Decay: 0.01
  - Floating Point Precision: IEEE FP32 across all runs (eliminating FP16 gradient underflow NaNs)
  - Random Seed: `42`
  - Validation-Based Model Selection: Best epoch checkpoint saved based on validation Macro F1.

---

## 8. Model Architectures

1. **Legal-RoBERTa** (`saibo/legal-roberta-base`): RoBERTa model family pre-trained on legal domain corpora (court cases, legislation, contracts).
2. **Legal-BERT** (`nlpaueb/legal-bert-base-uncased`): BERT variant pre-trained on European and US legal contracts and legislation.
3. **DeBERTa-v3** (`microsoft/deberta-v3-base`): State-of-the-art general-domain encoder featuring disentangled attention and enhanced mask decoder.

---

## 9. Loss Functions and Class Imbalance

1. **Standard BCE** (`BCEWithLogitsLoss`): Evaluates unweighted binary cross-entropy across all 14 categories.
2. **Multi-Label Focal Loss** (gamma=2.0, alpha=0.25): Down-weights easy negative examples to focus gradients on hard examples.
3. **Class-Weighted BCE**: Applies per-class positive loss weight w_c = N_neg,c / N_pos,c derived **exclusively** from the training split.

Strict leakage prevention ensured validation and test label counts were never used in weight calculations.

---

## 10. Evaluation Protocol

- **Primary Benchmark Metric**: **Macro F1** (unweighted mean of F1 scores across all 14 legal categories).
- **Secondary Metrics**: Minority Macro F1 (7 minority classes), Multi-Label MCC, Micro F1, Weighted F1, Hamming Loss.
- **Reporting Rule**: All metrics reported to 4 decimal places in tables; test split accessed **once** per experiment.

## 15. Discussion

1. **Why Class Weighting Mattered**: Unweighted BCE and Focal Loss failed because standard binary cross-entropy gradients from massive negative support overwhelm minority positive gradients. Class weighting rebalances gradient magnitudes, forcing the encoder to learn minority features.
2. **Why Confidence Filtering Reduced Performance**: Distant supervision label noise is bounded. Dropping lower-confidence clauses stripped 41.57% of positive training examples for minority categories, severely degrading minority recall.
3. **Why Threshold 0.60 Improved Classifier Performance**: Class weighting scales positive logits upward, creating a slight overprediction bias at threshold 0.50. Shifting the decision threshold to 0.60 restored precision without compromising recall.

## 18. Conclusion

Among the evaluated configurations, **`Legal-RoBERTa` with Class-Weighted BCE (`EXP-03`)** achieved superior multi-label NDA clause classification performance (Test Macro F1 = **0.3675** at threshold 0.50). Validation-based threshold optimization to **0.60** elevated Test Macro F1 to **0.4295** and Minority Macro F1 to **0.3853** while cutting false positives by 46.9%. Retaining all valid pseudo-labeled training clauses proved superior to confidence filtering. These findings offer practical design principles for building imbalance-aware legal NLP systems.
